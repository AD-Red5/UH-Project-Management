## 4. What I kept vs. what I changed

### My own decisions retained in the project

I made the major product and business decisions through the question-and-answer process. The final draft retains my decisions that:

- WE ARE $oftware ¢orp. is a small Houston startup pursuing a parking-market opportunity.
- The pilot customer is **Bayou City Parking**, operating several garages in a downtown stadium district.
- ParkPilot's core value is showing vacancy or percent full and helping drivers reserve parking ahead of events.
- The product will use an interactive map with walking distance to events.
- Availability should update at least once per minute.
- The product will support a website and mobile apps, cards, Apple Pay, and Google Pay.
- Paid reservations are held for 15 minutes; additional time requires a paid extension.
- Cancellations made at least one hour before arrival receive a full refund.
- The project includes demand-based pricing, but it may move only up or down 25% from the normal operator-set rate and must show the final price before checkout.
- EV-charging parking is included in Version 1; accessible spaces are displayed but cannot be reserved online.

### AI-assisted material retained

I retained the AI-generated SRS organization, requirement/use-case structure, initial draft wording, and the summary comparison of existing parking platforms. I reviewed the draft and supplied the product choices listed above before it was finalized as Version 0.1.

### Changes made during the AI-assisted drafting process

I changed the proposed scope through my answers. In particular, I changed dynamic pricing from an initially deferred feature to a Version 1 feature with a strict ±25% cap, included EV-charging reservations in Version 1, and specified that accessible spaces would be visible but not reservable online.
